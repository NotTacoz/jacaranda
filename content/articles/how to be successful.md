having recently read [sam altman's blogs](https://blog.samaltman.com/advice-for-ambitious-19-year-olds) on success and ambition, it inspired me to think a lot about my future and reflect where *i* am right now - something which i have buried under the workloads of school and studying and exams. i would describe myself to me "ambitious" to an extent (that i want to lead a life that is meaningful and above average), but this "ambition" does not necessarily correlate with what i choose for my future, and i am realising that.

tldr is that i have come to the horrific realisation that in my ambitions to lead a successful and "above average" life, i find myself doing the most normal and below average use of time in order to get there. this has inspired lots of self-reflection on where i am and traits i want to possess to be "there" in life.

this article is obviously not a 100% definite way to be successful, but are the core traits of what i would define a successful person to have, and would make or break a person in order to be "successful".

1. compounding risk, and compounding rewards
exponential curves and compounding is a beautiful sight. but most pathways and careers are linear. this is the dichotomy of the successful and the average, because the average follows a linear (though sometimes exponential) pathway (e.g. income, increasing by a set number every 2 years) to where they are, however, successful people, compound everything.

i believe compounding is a result of taking massive risk for a long time for an even greater reward. 1 financial quarter makes millionaires into billionaires and 1 financial quarters makes a [few billion dollars in net worth into 100 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/billionaires/profiles/jenhsun-huang/?embedded-checkout=true).

point is, many things that you do has unforeseen value in the next year, 5 years, 10 years. it is impossibly time wasting to judge this value, because by the time you have finished judging it. you've missed out the opportunity.
