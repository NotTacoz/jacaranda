## titrations
- basically are used to find unknown concentration in experiment
- quantitative (technically qualitative)
- fill up burette
- measure change in volume
## indicators
- protonated and non protonated form
- they are complex and change colours over narrow pH change.
- equivalence point is when stoichiometrically there is exact same amount of acid and base
- memorise methyl orange(acidic), phenolphthalein (basic)
- $HInd +H_{2}O\rightleftharpoons H_{3}O^++Ind^-$
## titration curves
- plot of pH of analyte solution versus volume of titrant added as titration progresses
- strong acid weak base: pH is exactly at 7
- end point -> indicator indicates (end point should ideally be at equivalent point)

## good indicator properties
- distinct noticeable change
- change colour over a narrow pH range
- have an end-point range that is near to our equivalence point.

## volumetric analysis definitions
- analyte: solution of unknown concentration
- aliquot: accurately measured volume/solution - whatever placed beneath the burette
- titrant: solution in the burette and volume used to reach end point. titre value (volume of titrant) should be within 0.2 ml of each other.
