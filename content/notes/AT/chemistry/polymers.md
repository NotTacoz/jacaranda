### intermolecular forces
- most addition polymers are hydrocarbons - non-polar and can ONLY form DISPERSION forces
- benzene rings can add to the total strength of of dispersion forces formed.
- if there is more branching present, such as in low density polyethene increase the spacing between the carbon chains, which decreases the density of carbon atoms and the strength of the intermolecular forces formed.
- polar polymers allow formation of dipole dipole forces.

## disposal

## polymer properties
edging into material science
- macromolecules - very long
- you can change the length of polymer chains
	- short - weak
	- long - strong
- all polymers form dispersion forces
	- because polymers are very long
	- polymers' dispersion forces are very strong !
- benzene adds extra strength to the dispersion forces
- c-cl adds extra imf if youve got the right functional group
- however, **most** polymers are held together by dispersion forces
- condensation polymers adds other imfs
	- all condensation can do dipole dipoles
	- because polyesters and polyamides can form d-d- forces
	- polyamides can form h bonds too 
## crystalline
- areas where the chains of polymers line up parallel.
	- produces highest strength of intermolecular forces
	- so the chains stick together
- amorphous
	- randomly ordered molecular structure
- branching affects the amount of crystalline areas produced - the more brancing there is, the less amount of crystalline areas.
- hdpe lots of crystalline (95%)
- ldpe less  crystalline than hdpe (65%)

## heating polymers
- polymers are brittle when cold (glassy)
- when they ware warm, they become flexible, or elastic (not stretchy elastic, its like flexible elastic)
- no clear drop off point, there is a **gradual** transition between the polymers
- solid -> flexible -> squidgy and soft -> liquid
## critical length
- threshold for polymer to behave like a polymer
- polyethene: 100 carbons long
	- if its less than that, it will be like an alkane fr

## copolymerising
- nonomers with side groups protrude from the main chain
- copolymerising - branches sometimes but not every time

## cross-linking
- thermoplastics - reshaped upon heating
- thermosetting polymers - fake plastic: set and shape cannot be made squishy
	- cannot be melted back into a liquid
- elastomers: occasional cross links between chains. stretchy! but crosslinks pull back to its original position

## additives
- plasticisers : space polymer chains to increase flexibility
- uv stabiliser: prevent sun damage (degradation)
- gases: polymer into a foam
- pigments: increase opacity and/or colours
