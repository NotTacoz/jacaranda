## properties of arenes
arenes: aromatic hydrocarbons that contain one or more benzene rings
- aromatic derives from strong-smelling substances in nature
- benzene colourless liquid at room temperature
- boiling point s comparable to hexane but melting point is much higher
- because ability of flat benzene rings to pack closely together when solid, increasing strength of dispersion forces formed.
- immiscible in water.
- because the electrons are packed closer together, strength of dispersion forces increase.
## reactivity
- due to stability of delocalised ring structure, do not readily take part in typical alkene reaction
- no silly addition
- aromatics are better for substitution as it allows product to retain stability of benzene ring

## carboxylic acids
- carboxyl group consisting of carbonyl group and a hydroxyl group.
- properties of carboxylic acid
	- relatively high melting and boiling points
	- very soluble in water
- exaggerate polarity of the carbonyl and hydroxyl group.
- the link is called a dimer (not permanent)
- as chains get longer, becomes get less soluble in water.
- weak acids: some ionise, helps solubility.
- dense for organic substances
	- long chain ones sometimes is the bottom layer and the water is the top layer.
### reactions of carboxylic acids 
- neutralisation
	- carboxylic acid loses a proton to form a carboxylate salt.
	- forms carboxylate ion (ethanoic acid -> ethanoate)
	- negative charge is delocalised across carboxylate group, resulting in a more stable ion - delocalisation is represented by adding a second dotted line to the carbon oxygen bonds, which are both equivalent. TLDR; double bond is shared between the two oxygen rather than 1 double bonded Oxygen 1 single bond (as shown in diagram)
- nucleophilic substitution
- esterification
	- subtype of substitution

## esters

condensed formula is written as COO
carboxylic acid + alcohol -> ester + water
- esters are named after alcohol and carboxylic acid they are made from.
- alcohol gives the first part of the name, carboxylic acid gives the second. always ends in "-anoate"
### properties
- ester functional group is polar - esters can form d-d forces as well as dispersion forces.
- can have oxygen lone pairs - dont have hydrogen bonding but can form hydrogen bond between WATER and ester! :-)
- esters:
	- slow reactions
	- reversible reaction
	- heated to make faster
		- but not too hot otherwise it will be gas
		- so warm is good, not HOT
	- catalyst added (concentrated sulphuric acid)
- equilibrium
	- slow and reversible
	- ester has LOWEST boiling point, so if you boil everything and have ester as the only evaporated product, there is no more ester left to do the reverse reaction (because it evaporates away bye bye)
	- also use as little water as possible - no aqueous solutions use neat (very concentrated) - water is NOT the solvent
